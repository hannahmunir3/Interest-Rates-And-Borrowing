# UK Interest Rates and Household Debt: Evidence from 2019-2025

# Project Overview

This project examines how UK household finances changed as interest rates increased between 2019-2025.

The analysis investigates the relationship between the bank rate, household debt to income ratio, household saving ratio and real disposable income per head using quartely UK data.

I am to identify key trends and relationships in household financial behaviour from 2019 to 2025.

# Key Findings

• Bank rate fell from 0.75% in 2019 to around 0.10% during 2020-2021 before increasing rapidly to 5.25% in 2023.

• The household debt to income ratio increased during the 2020-2021 period.

• Real disposable income per head declined during parts of 2022-2023 before recovering during 2024.

• The COVID-19 period represents an important unusual period in the data, particularly because of the sharp increase in household saving.

• The household savings ratio increased sharply during the COVID-19 period, before returning closer to its long-term range.

# Data

The analysis uses quartely UK data covering 2019 Q1 to 2025 Q4.

The following variables are measured in percentage:

• Bank rate

• Debt to income

• Savings ratio


Real disposable income is measure in GBP (£).


## Data Sources

• Bank rate - The Bank of England

• Household Debt to Income ratio - Office for National Statisitcs

• Household saving ratio - Office for National Statisitcs

• Real disposable income - Office for National Statisitcs


# Methodology

The project follows this workflow:

Raw data, Cleaning, Analysis, Visualisation

### 1. Raw Data 

Quartely data was collected from the Bank of England and Office for National Statistics covering 2019 Q1-2025 Q4.

#### 2. Cleaning

The datasets were combined into a single quartely dataset. Variables we checked for consistency, missing observations and appropriate units before analysis.


### 3. Analysis

The analysis examined:

• Trends in bank rate

• Changes in household debt to income ratio

• Changes in the household savings ratio

• Changes in the real disposable income per head

• The relationship between bank rate and household debt to income

• Correlation between selected variables


### Visualisation

The analysis uses scatter plots to identify trends and relationships between interest rates and household financial indicators.

This can be found in the jupyter notebok labelled "household_debt_analysis.ipynb".

# Limitations

• The analysis does not establish causation.

• The COVID-19 period represents an unusual economic shock and may affect comparisons across a larger data set.

• The data set cover a small time period of seven years.

• The bank rate does not represent the actual interest rate paid by every household. Additionally, many mortgage borrowers have fixed-rate agreements.

• Household borrowing and savings are influenced by many different factors.
